Business logic usually dies by a thousand rewrites: the same conditional rules expressed in a form library, a backend validator, a workflow engine, and a spreadsheet, each drifting from the others. BLOP treats business rules the way Terraform treats infrastructure — declared once in a universal intermediate representation, diffed, planned, and applied. mation-engine is the execution layer that runs the IR.

The IR is the load-bearing idea. Conditional logic — eligibility, pricing, approval chains, escalation — compiles down to a single declarative graph with explicit dependencies between rules. Changing a rule produces a plan: what changes, what it touches, what breaks. That is the Terraform discipline applied to the layer where businesses actually keep their behavior.

Written in TypeScript so the same engine runs in the browser form, the Node backend, and the edge worker — one IR, three runtimes, no drift. The schema layer, Table-Schema, gives the IR its typed contract so a rule that references a field that no longer exists fails at plan time, not in production.
