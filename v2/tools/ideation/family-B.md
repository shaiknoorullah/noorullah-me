### ★ 1. Hot Aisle, 03:47
- **Scene:** one endless data-center hot aisle at night — a single row of black server racks stretching into darkness, green status LEDs breathing in slow waves, volumetric heat shimmer rising off the tops.
- **Scroll story:** dolly down the aisle at eye height → truck sideways across the LED wall → crane up into the cable-tray ceiling → macro of one blinking NIC port → top-down of the aisle as a glowing circuit trace → pull back to reveal the aisle is one line in a vast dark hall.
- **Pillar(s):** Composed under load — thousands of machines working, nothing hurrying, everything monitored; the calm IS the story.
- **Asset anchors:** "Server Rack" — Shalmon, 74,823 tris; "Server rack" — Osho, 29,711 tris; "Ladder Cable Tray" — AlfazalEng, 30,104 tris (ceiling run).
- **Cohesion mechanism:** one environment, one repeated modular subject, one light story (LED green + ember fans + cool UPS fill).
- **Polish hook:** the macro act — a single SFP port where each LED blink releases one green photon-pulse that travels up the fiber and out of frame; 99.999% rendered as rhythm, not text.
- **Risk:** datacenter renders are everywhere on Dribbble — needs the "night-shift, nobody here, everything fine" stillness and film-grade volumetrics to escape cliché.

## Self-rating (top 3 marked ★ above)

1. **★ Hot Aisle, 03:47** — highest personal relevance (his daily world, the 99.999% story told as stillness), strongest 6-act fit, most buildable at 60fps.
2. **★ The Landing Station** — the most distinctive subject in the family; literalizes "substrate over rent" better than any datacenter interior; one-cable cohesion is elegant.
3. **★ The Mesh Pavilion** — the purest "network topology as architecture" play and the only one that tells the agent-platform/leverage story visually; biggest cliché risk but biggest payoff if executed physically.

Runners-up: The Meet-Me Room (quiet, insider-authentic), The Splice (best single macro moment), The Dark NOC (best leverage narrative but most cliché-prone).

**Family's strongest angle:** the winning concepts don't depict "networks" generically — they pick ONE physical place where signal becomes matter (a cable landing, an aisle, a splice, a suspended graph) and let one green pulse carry the camera through it. The pulse-as-dolly-track device is the family's unifying cinematographic signature and its best defense against the "stitched objects" rejection.
